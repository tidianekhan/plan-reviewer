# Pellerby Lantern Festival

Plan from the Lantern Festival Committee for the Town Council events office.
Written up by Hannah Cole, committee secretary, on behalf of the committee.

## Summary

This will be the third Pellerby Lantern Festival. On the evening of the festival, families walk with lanterns made in the town's two primary schools from Mill Lane School, down the High Street and along the harbour to Quay Green. The Pellerby Silver Band leads the parade, and on the green there is a samba band, food stalls, and the finale, when all the lanterns are hung together on a big frame and lit up at once. Last year about 800 people came, and we would like to make room for more this time.

The festival is on the evening of day 45, counting from our kickoff meeting, and everything on the task list below has to be done by then.

The committee is the same five people as last year. Ruth Marsh, our chair, has very kindly agreed to look after the permissions and the bookings again. She did all of this last year and knows everyone at the council, and it saves a lot of back and forth if one person keeps it all together. Tom does the publicity, Priya runs the lantern workshops, Gareth looks after the site, and Hannah looks after the money and the volunteers.

## The evening itself

The parade gathers in the Mill Lane School playground from 5pm and sets off at 5.30, just as it is getting dark. The High Street is closed to traffic from 5pm to 7pm while the parade goes through. On Quay Green, the samba band plays from 6pm, the food stalls are open until 9pm, and the finale is at 8pm. The green is clear by 10pm.

## Notes on the tasks

### Permissions and safety

Ruth will walk the route with Sandra Pike from the council's highways team, and they will agree the site plan for Quay Green together: where the stage, the food stalls, the toilets and the finale frame go, and where the barriers are needed along the harbour wall.

The road closure is the biggest single job. The council's form asks for a traffic management plan, a map of the closure points, and a copy of the letter that goes to every house and business on the High Street. Ruth did it last year, so she knows what they want.

Gareth is talking to the Pellerby First Responders about first aid cover. Last year they sent two volunteers and a first aid tent, and they have said they would be happy to come again.

Ruth will write the risk assessment once Gareth has confirmed the first aid cover, because the council's template asks for the first aid arrangements to be written into it.

We will take out the event insurance through the same broker as last year. Last year they would not give us a quote until they had seen the finished risk assessment, so Ruth will do the insurance after that.

### Music and food

The Silver Band has led the parade every year, and the Quayside Samba drummers have asked to play on the green again. Ruth will book the bands and agree dates and fees with both.

Once both bands are booked and have sent over what they need, Ruth will also arrange the hire of the stage lighting and a PA system for the green. She reckons that is three days of phone calls and quotes.

For food, Ruth will invite the traders who came last year and fill any gaps from the market list, up to twelve stalls on the pitches shown on the site plan. We have asked for at least one stall with hot drinks and one with vegetarian food.

### Lanterns

The lanterns are what the festival is about. Hannah will write to the businesses on the High Street and around the harbour for sponsorship, and we cannot order the lantern-making materials until that money is in. The materials are willow canes, tissue paper, masking tape and battery tea lights. We do not use real candles.

Priya will run after-school lantern-making workshops at Mill Lane School and Harbour Road School, with about twenty children in each session. Parents are welcome to stay and help.

When the workshops finish, Priya will collect the finished lanterns from both schools and store them at the community centre until the evening, which takes her two days.

Gareth will build the frame for the finale in his garage, so it only needs putting up on the green on the evening. He can start as soon as the sponsorship money is in to buy the timber, and it takes him eight days.

### Publicity

The poster and flyer name both bands, so Tom will start the design once the bands are booked. The printers cannot have the poster until the road closure has been approved, because the poster has the route map and the closure times on it.

Once the poster design is done, Tom will also write the press release for the Pellerby Echo and set up the posts for our social media page, so that everything uses the same artwork. He thinks that is three days.

Tom will put the posters up in the shops, the library and the surgery, and deliver the flyers to both schools and to every house on the route.

### Stewards and volunteers

We need around thirty stewards along the route and on the green. Ruth will recruit them, mostly from last year's list and the sailing club, once the route is agreed and we know how many points need covering. She will do the steward briefing herself once the risk assessment is written, so that everyone knows the closure points, the first aid tent and what to do if a child gets lost.

Once Ruth has recruited the stewards, Hannah will draw up the volunteer rota, covering the stewards, the helpers at the gathering point and the people looking after the finale frame.

### Site

Gareth will hire the toilets and the crowd barriers once the site plan is agreed. Last year the council's waste team lent us extra bins for the green, and we expect to ask for the same again.

## Money

We are hoping to raise £2,500 in sponsorship, and the council's small events grant gives us £500. The biggest costs are the lantern materials, the road closure, the toilets and barriers, and the stage lighting. Any money left over goes towards next year's festival.
